    Adapter Header¶

    The adapter header is a 64-pin keyed box header, 2 × 32 on 2.54 mm, marked ADAPTER on the mainboard. A 64-way ribbon connects it pin-for-pin to the adapter card, or to your own panel. The motor bus travels separately on the XT30.

    Pin 1 is the square pad at the top left of the header. Odd pins are on the top row, even pins below, and the key notch is on the odd-pin side. Every differential pair sits across one column, so the pairs stay together on the ribbon.

    5V_X and 24V_X are the mainboard's 5 V and 24 V rails through their own 1.1 A PTC fuses, for sensors and small loads on the adapter side. GND is the field ground.

    Signal groups¶

    Motion, columns 1 to 32. For each axis a step pair, a direction pair and an alarm pair. Enable is one pair shared by all five axes, on pins 3 and 4. See Motors.

    Machine I/O, spread between the axes. Limit inputs each sit beside a fused 5 V pin so a sensor can be powered from the same column. Probe, tool setter, RS-485, the spindle lines and the coolant switches complete the set.

    ENA − is a driven output

    Pins 3 and 4 are the two halves of an RS-422 pair. Never tie ENA_N to ground on an adapter or panel: four paralleled driver outputs would be shorted whenever the enable is high. For a single-ended enable use ENA_P against GND and leave ENA_N unconnected. See Motors.

    Using the header without an adapter¶

    The DIY adapter brings every pin to screw terminals. If you build your own panel instead, use a keyed 64-way IDC ribbon and observe the polarity notes above. Keep the ribbon under a metre, and keep it away from the VFD cable.
